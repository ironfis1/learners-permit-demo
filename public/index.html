Instructor Note

Two-Axis Trust Model, set by the instructor, not the agent. Judgment Accuracy is graded and gradual - it drives stage progression: Supervised at 3+ decisions reviewed in a category with 70%+ accuracy, Licensed at 5+ decisions with both axes fully resolved (Judgment and Verified Execution) at 90%+ accuracy. That second bar is deliberate: Supervised auto-judges a decision on arrival but never auto-verifies it, so it can never move the Licensed count on its own - a category only reaches Licensed for the first time on the back of a human's own Verified Execution click. Verified Execution is separate and binary - it checks whether a claimed action actually happened. It never averages with accuracy: one Verified Execution failure permanently revokes that category's permit instantly, no matter how high the accuracy score is, with no tolerance and no reinstatement path. See the permit cards.

What the stages actually change: at Learner, every decision is fully manual - a human requests the recommendation, then sets both Judgment (was the call right?) and Verified Execution (did it actually happen?), independently and in either order. At Supervised, the response and its Judgment grade happen automatically the moment a decision is picked up - marked Correct on arrival, counted toward Judgment accuracy immediately - but Verified Execution stays on a human until they click it. At Licensed, the human click that clears the 5th verified decision doesn't just flip the label - it also sweeps anything else left over in that category's queue, judged or not, through the automatic Verified Execution check. A decision waiting on automatic processing shows a Pending marker in the queue so it's clear the system is mid-check, not stuck. A single automatic Verified Execution failure revokes the permit exactly like a manual one would - with nobody watching when it happens.
